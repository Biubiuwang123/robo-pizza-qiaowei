Scene 3 · Keeping secrets out of sight

The locked box

To order pizza, Robo has to log in to the pizza shop's website. That means Robo needs a password, and a password is only useful while it stays secret.

1 The password on the instruction card

This is how Robo was first set up: the password is written right into its instructions.

Robo's instructions

  1. Open the pizza shop's website
  2. Log in as maple-hill-party, password pepperoni-42
  3. Order 6 large, half cheese

14 club members copied the instructions. One posted them on the club's website. A search engine found the page.

Overnight: 9 pizza orders nobody at Maple Hill placed.

🤖 Instructions get copied, shared, and posted. Anything written in them is no longer secret.

A password written into the instructions is a hard-coded secret. It happens so often that code-hosting sites scan for them.

2 Who may open the box?

The box keeps the password scrambled. Only the key keeper can unscramble it, and only for someone on the list.

3 Change the combination

Passwords should change now and then, and right away if one leaks. Robo orders a pizza every couple of seconds. Change the password and keep the orders going.

Peek at a real locked box

This scene, for real, in AWS Secrets Manager: a box created, two names checked against the list by AWS's own policy evaluator, the combination changed, and everything deleted afterwards. The password was random and only its first letter is shown.

# 1. Put the pizza shop's password in the box
$ aws secretsmanager create-secret --name robo-pizza/pizza-shop-login
   ARN: arn:aws:secretsmanager:us-east-1:<account>:secret:robo-pizza/pizza-shop-login-<id>
   Encrypted with: aws/secretsmanager (AWS managed key)

# 2. The list: Robo may open the box, the hall monitor may not
   Robo          GetSecretValue on the box -> allowed
   Hall monitor  GetSecretValue on the box -> implicitDeny
   (evaluated by IAM's policy simulator; anything not explicitly allowed is denied)

# 3. Ask the box at the moment the password is needed
$ aws secretsmanager get-secret-value --secret-id robo-pizza/pizza-shop-login

The smallest locked box that works

Ticks as you play

  1. Keeps the secret out of the instructions
  2. Keeps it scrambled, with the key kept elsewhere
  3. Opens only for those on the list
  4. Writes every attempt in a logbook
  5. Hands the secret over at the moment it's needed
  6. Changes the combination without a human
  7. Labels old and new, so nothing breaks mid-change

That's the whole product. Everything else is scale and polish.
